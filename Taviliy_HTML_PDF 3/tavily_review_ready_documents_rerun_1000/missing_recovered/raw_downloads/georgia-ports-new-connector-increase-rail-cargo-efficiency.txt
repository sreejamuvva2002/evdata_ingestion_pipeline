# Georgia Ports to Increase Rail Cargo Efficiency with New Connector

## The project will create local jobs and provide efficient connections to the Port of Savannah

![Image 21: GPA's Blue Ridge Connector project will boost Georgia's rail cargo capacity, linking Northeast Georgia with global container ship services at the Port of Savannah.](https://www.supplychain247.com/images/2020_article/georgia-port2.jpg)

 By  24/7 Staff August 8, 2024

[The Georgia Ports Authority (GPA)](https://georgia.gov/organization/georgia-ports-authority#:~:text=Established%20in%201945%20by%20an,inland%20river%20ports%20within%20Georgia.) is expanding its [rail](https://www.supplychain247.com/topic/category/rail) cargo capacity to enhance [container movement](https://www.supplychain247.com/topic/category/pallets) across the state and into the Midwest. GPA General Manager of Inland Operations, Wesley Barrell, announced progress on the Blue Ridge Connector near Gainesville, Ga., during the Greater Hall Chamber of Commerce Logistics Forum.

The 104-acre site will feature six tracks totaling 18,000 feet and create 20 local jobs. The inland [port](https://www.supplychain247.com/topic/tag/Ports)will connect Northeast Georgia with the Port of Savannah’s global container ship services. Norfolk Southern Railroad will provide a five-day-a-week service, operating from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m., Monday through Friday. The GPA aims for expedited service from Savannah to the Blue Ridge Connector with third-day availability.

“We will offer refrigerated cargo by rail, with this unique transit from the Blue Ridge Connector to Savannah,” he said. “There are five cold storage freezers within five miles of our location, and more than 50 users of those cold storage facilities. We look to provide services to this customer base.”

The facility, slated to open in 2026, is expected to cost $127 million and will serve industries like heavy equipment and forest products. The Blue Ridge Connector will act as an economic catalyst, similar to GPA’s Appalachian Regional Port (ARP), which has significantly boosted the local economy.

A new foreign trade zone near the Blue Ridge Connector will offer additional flexibility for cargo owners. The inland port will provide a competitive [transportation](https://www.supplychain247.com/topic/category/transportation) option with [reduced carbon emissions per container.](https://www.supplychain247.com/topic/category/sustainability) This will minimize truck traffic on state highways, particularly through the Atlanta area.

The Blue Ridge Connector will have an annual capacity of 200,000 containers and will significantly enhance GPA’s rail strategy. Supported by the Port of Savannah’s Mason Mega Rail Terminal, which has doubled intermodal capacity to 2 million TEUs per year, the Blue Ridge Connector is part of GPA's effort to speed cargo flow to inland markets.
